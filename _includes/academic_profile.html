Prof. Charalambos (Haris) Themistocleous is a full Professor of Speech, Language, and Communication at the University of Oslo. His research investigates the neurobiological and computational foundations of human language and cognition, focusing on how subtle acoustic, prosodic, morphosyntactic, and semantic patterns in natural communication can be decoded to assess and monitor brain health.

Bridging clinical neurolinguistics, experimental phonetics, neuroimaging, and artificial intelligence, Prof. Themistocleous pioneers explainable, transdiagnostic digital language biomarkers for the early detection, differential subtyping, prognosis, and treatment-efficacy evaluation of neurodegenerative and neurodevelopmental conditions—including Primary Progressive Aphasia (PPA), Mild Cognitive Impairment (MCI), Alzheimer’s disease (AD), stroke-induced aphasia, Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD), and Developmental Language Disorder (DLD).

Prior to joining the University of Oslo faculty, he conducted postdoctoral research in computational neurolinguistics at the Johns Hopkins University School of Medicine (Department of Neurology, 2018–2022), computational linguistics at the University of Gothenburg (Centre for Linguistic Theory and Studies in Probability – CLASP, 2015–2017), and as a Visiting Research Scholar at Princeton University (2015). He is the founder and lead architect of Open Brain AI, an open-access multilingual computational platform automating clinical speech, language, and discourse analysis across 15+ languages for researchers, clinicians, and educators across Europe and North America.

Frontier Research Vision & European Research Landscape

Our research group develops information-theoretic, neurobiologically grounded, and cross-linguistically generalizable AI architectures that move clinical diagnostics beyond subjective testing and English-centric "black-box" models. By integrating connected speech acoustics, large language model embeddings, and multimodal neuroimaging (fMRI, pCASL perfusion, DTI, and tDCS neuromodulation), we aim to establish a scalable European and global paradigm for precision neuro-diagnostics.
